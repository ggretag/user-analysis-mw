 Here is why 10 minutes is the "sweet spot" for your specific project:

  1. The "Actionability" Rule
  Your goal is to have an AI assistant that helps users inside the UI. 
   * If a user is inactive for 25 minutes, they are likely reading a physical document, talking on the phone, or in another app
     (Excel/Outlook). Your UI assistant cannot help them with those "outside" tasks.
   * 10 minutes captures the time they are actually "wrestling" with your platform. This is the only time the AI can actually
     intervene and save them clicks.

  2. High-Density vs. Low-Density Work
   * 10-minute data (High Density): Shows you the "Work Bench." It identifies where users are actively clicking, scrolling, and
     entering data. These are the areas where a "Co-pilot" or "Auto-filler" has the highest ROI.
   * 30-minute data (Low Density): Shows you "Engagement." It’s a good metric for a Sales team to say "Users stay in our app all
     day," but it’s a bad metric for an Engineering team trying to fix bottlenecks. It hides the bottlenecks under a layer of "idle
     noise."

  3. The "Struggle" Threshold
  In a complex trade platform:
   * 2 minutes of silence: Normal. They are thinking or checking a value.
   * 5 minutes of silence: Long. They might be looking up a complex rule in the documentation.
   * 10 minutes of silence: Disconnected. They have almost certainly shifted their focus away from the screen.

  Summary Recommendation:
   * Use 10 minutes for your Business Case for AI. (e.g., "We can automate 20% of these 90 high-intensity hours").
   * Use 30 minutes only if you are calculating System Licensing or "Daily Active Usage" (how long the app is kept open).

  Final Verdict: 10 minutes gives you "Clean Truth." 30 minutes gives you "Polished Statistics." For a technical analysis like this,
  the truth is more useful.
